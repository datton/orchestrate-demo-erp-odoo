Odoo as an example

Odoo is a business software suite made of apps that work together, which makes it a good example of an ERP. This page describes it in general terms.

How it is organised

Odoo is modular. An organisation turns on the apps it needs, and they share the same customer, product and accounting data. Odoo has a free community edition and a paid enterprise edition.

Example app areas

  • Sales and CRM

    managing leads, quotations and customer orders.

  • Accounting and Invoicing

    customer invoices, vendor bills and payments.

  • Inventory

    stock levels and deliveries.

  • Purchase

    ordering from suppliers.

  • Manufacturing

    producing goods from components.

  • Point of Sale

    selling in a shop.

  • Website and eCommerce

    an online presence and online selling.

  • Project

    planning and tracking tasks.

  • Employees and Time Off

    basic HR records and leave.

Example workflow: order to cash

This is a general description of how an order moves through connected apps, from first quote to payment.

  1. Quotation: sales prepares a quotation for the customer.
  2. Sales order: when the customer accepts, the quotation is confirmed as a sales order.
  3. Delivery: inventory prepares and ships the goods, and stock is updated.
  4. Invoice: accounting creates the customer invoice from the order.
  5. Payment: the customer pays and the payment is matched to the invoice.

Because the apps share data, each step starts from the previous one instead of being re-entered.
